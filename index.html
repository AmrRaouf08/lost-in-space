LST IN SPACE

Left behind. Never forgotten.

More than fifty years ago, astronauts drove a buggy across the Moon. Since then, robots have rolled across Mars and even flown over it. When their work was done, they stayed right where they stopped, still and silent. Fly with us to meet them and hear what they found.

The Moon

Narrator

On the Moon, even a short walk was hard work. Bulky spacesuits, rough ground and limited time made exploring a real challenge.

Three Lunar Roving Vehicles went to the Moon, folded flat inside the lander. Astronauts drove them on Apollo 15, 16 and 17. They had wire-mesh wheels, no roof, and no air.

With the rover on the surface, the astronauts could travel farther, explore more of the lunar landscape and reach places they could not easily visit on foot.

Together they carried astronauts about 90 km, much farther than they could walk in bulky spacesuits, to reach far-away rocks and bring them home.Fun fact: they folded up like a suitcase to fit in the rocket.

When the astronauts left the Moon, the rover stayed behind. Its tracks still mark the journeys of Apollo 15, 16 and 17.

Then the astronauts flew home. The buggies stayed. Nobody has driven them since 1972. With no wind or rain, their tracks can last a very long time.

Mars: The Twins

Narrator

Two robotic explorers arrived on Mars with one big question: had water ever changed this dry, dusty world?

Spirit landed on 3 January 2004 in Gusev Crater. Opportunity landed on 24 January 2004 on the other side of Mars, at Meridiani Planum. Each rover is about the size of a golf cart.

Both had 90-day missions. Spirit worked for more than six years, Opportunity for more than 14!

Their journeys took them to different parts of Mars. Each rover followed its own path, searching the rocks and soil for clues about the planet’s past.

Spirit found rocks that formed in hot water long ago. That means this spot may once have been a good home for tiny living things. We still don’t know if any lived there.Fun fact: a broken wheel dragged through the dirt and dug up bright, water-made soil.

Opportunity found rocks and tiny round pebbles that formed in water. It’s strong evidence that salty water once covered this area.Fun fact: scientists nicknamed the pebbles “blueberries”.

The twins found different clues about Mars’s watery past. Their missions ended in different ways, but together they changed what we know about Mars.

Spirit got stuck in soft sand in 2009. Winter came, its solar panels couldn’t catch enough sun, and it went quiet in March 2010.

Opportunity was covered by a giant dust storm in 2018. Its solar panels went dark. Its last message came on 10 June 2018.People often quote it as “My battery is low and it’s getting dark”. That’s a poetic translation of the data, not its literal words.

Mars: in the air

Narrator

After travelling to Mars attached to Perseverance, a tiny helicopter was ready to attempt something no machine had ever done on another planet.

Ingenuity is a helicopter that weighs only 1.8 kg. It rode to Mars on the belly of the Perseverance rover and landed in February 2021.

Flying on Mars was no ordinary challenge. Its thin atmosphere offered very little lift, so Ingenuity had to spin its rotors at extraordinary speed.

Mars air is about 1% as thick as Earth’s, so its two rotors spin around 2,400 times a minute, just to get a grip.

On 19 April 2021 it made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet. It was only built to fly five times!Fun fact: it carries a tiny piece of fabric from the Wright brothers’ 1903 Flyer.

What began as a test of just five flights became 72. Ingenuity gave scientists a new aerial view of Mars and proved that powered flight was possible on another world.

It flew 72 times, scouting the ground ahead so Perseverance could drive safely.

On its last flight, 18 January 2024, its rotor blades were damaged. NASA ended the mission a week later. Ingenuity is still at Jezero Crater, right where it stopped.

They’re not forgotten.

A lunar buggy, two twin rovers and a tiny helicopter each finished their jobs on other worlds.

The buggies carried astronauts farther than they could walk. Spirit and Opportunity found clues that water once shaped Mars. Ingenuity showed that a helicopter can fly there. What they learned helped shape the missions that came after.

Machines can go quiet. Their stories don’t have to.
